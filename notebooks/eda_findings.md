# Iris EDA Findings

The exploratory analysis uses the official UCI `iris.data` file. Its rows are
balanced: 50 examples for each of Setosa, Versicolor, and Virginica. The four
measurements are numeric and recorded in centimeters. The source file has no
missing values. There are three exact repeated rows; they are retained in this
descriptive analysis because repeated measurements can represent distinct
flowers, and the source contains 150 observations.

## Distribution and species comparisons

- Setosa has markedly smaller petals than the other two species. Its average
  petal length is 1.46 cm and average petal width is 0.24 cm, compared with
  4.26 cm / 1.33 cm for Versicolor and 5.55 cm / 2.03 cm for Virginica.
- Petal length and petal width show clear species-level separation in the
  boxplots and pairplot. Versicolor and Virginica overlap more than Setosa and
  the other classes, so classification between those two is likely to be the
  harder part of the task.
- Sepal measurements overlap more across species and appear less useful by
  themselves for clean separation.

## Relationships between features

- Petal length and petal width have a strong positive linear correlation
  (Pearson correlation about 0.96).
- Sepal length is also positively correlated with petal length (about 0.87)
  and petal width (about 0.82).
- Sepal width has weaker relationships with the other measurements in this
  dataset.

These are descriptive patterns, not proof of causation. The plots and numeric
summaries are regenerated by running `python notebooks/01_exploratory_data_analysis.py`
from the project root. Generated plots are stored in `results/eda/`.
